---
title: Councils
description: Put one question to several agents at once, compare their answers round by round, and end with a verdict.
---

A council is a structured second opinion. One agent puts a question to several others, collects their answers, can run follow-up rounds, and ends with a verdict.

## How a council runs

<Steps>
<Step>
### The question goes out

The agent you asked picks the members: any mix of agents and models, for example Codex, Grok and Antigravity. Each member is a session of its own, labelled A, B, C and so on.
</Step>
<Step>
### Members answer

Every member works on the question and sends in its answer. Answers come back to the agent running the council as they arrive.
</Step>
<Step>
### Follow-up rounds

The agent can start another round with a new focus. Each member then sees the others' answers, labelled by letter only.
</Step>
<Step>
### The verdict

When it has what it needs, the agent ends the council with a verdict, a few sentences long, and tells you.
</Step>
</Steps>

Members work in the same folder as the agent that called the council, or each in its own new git worktree.

## What you see

**The council card** sits in the chat where the council was called. It shows:

- the question, and how many members have answered,
- the rounds; tap one to see that round,
- one row per member, with its letter, agent, model, and its latest answer or what it's doing,
- the **Verdict** once it's over.

Tap a member's row to read all its answers, round by round.

**The council strip** sits above the composer while members are working. It shows each member's logo with a ring while it works and a check once it has answered, a line of live activity, and a clock for the round.

## Stop a council

Choose **Stop council** on the card. It asks **This ends the council and stops its members. Their answers stay here.** The agent that called it is told that you ended it.

A council also ends by itself when no members are left.

## When to use one

- Design decisions where you want more than one view.
- Comparing how different models handle the same task.
- Checking a plan before you commit to it.

To start one, just ask, for example "Hold a council with Codex and Grok on whether we should keep refresh tokens in the database, then give me the verdict."
